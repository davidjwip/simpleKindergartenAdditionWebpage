# Scope map — HK K1–K3 and P1 mathematics, and where the apps fit

Compiled 26 Oct 2026 from the EDB primary sources already in this repo's `docs/`: the Kindergarten
Education Curriculum Guide 2017 (`[KG]`, §2.6.3 and Appendix 2) and the Explanatory Notes to Primary
Mathematics, Key Stage 1, 2018 (`[EN]`, units 1N1–1F1, with the P2/P3 markers that show what is
coming). Every quoted bound below is from those files.

Purpose: decide what the app estate should cover next, given that the current drill covers only part
of one P1 unit, and that a recent concept assessment points at halves.

---

## 1. What Hong Kong actually requires, K1 → P1

### 1.1 Kindergarten (K1–K3, ages 3–6)

There is no number syllabus. `[KG]` §2.6.3 sets one aim — "To develop children's interest and
motivation in learning mathematics through solving practical problems in real-life situations" — and
three objectives, of which the teachable content is:

- interest in numbers and quantities, and the concepts that number symbols represent;
- "the relationship between numbers and quantities", comparing and describing differences in quantity;
- properties of quantity, shape and space (front, back, left, right) explored "by various methods
  such as observation and touching";
- "sorting, ordering and sequencing";
- the concept of time and the regularity of days, months and seasons.

The only age-graded statements are Appendix 2 developmental milestones — not a curriculum:

| Band | Number | Shape / space / measure | Time and order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–4 | "recite from one to ten"; "count from one to three or four objects"; recall three numbers | match colours, shapes, sizes; sequence five rings by size; six-piece puzzles | — |
| 4–5 | "recite from one to twenty"; "count up to ten objects"; recall four things | eight-piece puzzles; distinguish front/back, fast/slow, middle/first/last | morning / afternoon / evening; what they usually do then |
| 5–6 | "count and write from one to twenty"; recall five numbers; "simple mathematical addition and subtraction" | order objects by length, height, width and size easily; name colours and shapes | **"Understand the difference between 'half of a piece' and 'one full piece'"**; first/second; year, month, day, week; o'clock |

Pedagogy is explicit and constrains any app: "One-way teaching and repetitive drilling on operations
of mathematics can cause resentment"; K2/K3 must not do "mechanical copying and calculation"; the
introduction of concepts should follow "simplicity and concreteness concentrating on one attribute at
a time and progressing gradually"; and pre-teaching primary content is prohibited.

### 1.2 Primary 1 (the twelve units, with their real bounds)

| Unit | Hours | What it requires | What it explicitly does **not** require |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1N1** Numbers to 20 | 13.5 | numerals 1–20 read/write; counting on and back (by 1 only); ordinal and cardinal; odd/even to 20; **"decomposition and composition of numbers 2-18"** from two 1-digit numbers | no `>` / `<` symbols; count ≤ 20 objects only |
| **1N2** Basic addition and subtraction | 13.5 | merging/adding and taking-away/comparing; **add/subtract "within 18 verbally"**; concept of 0; relation between add and sub; commutativity; problems "presented mainly by diagrams", recorded in horizontal form | no column form; **"operations such as 12+5 and 17–5 are not required"**; exploratory activities stay within 10 because "the objective is not for drilling of calculation" |
| **1N3** Numbers to 100 | 6 | 21–100 read/write; units and tens place; compare magnitudes and order them; count in groups of 2, 5, 10 ("calculation is not required"); estimate quantities < 100 | do not introduce the term "place value"; `>`/`<` only at 2N1 |
| **1N4** Addition and subtraction (I) | 13 | two-digit add (with carrying) and subtract (no borrowing); three-number addition; associative property; column form; simple word problems | results < 100; no written explanations of the calculation |
| **1M1** Length and distance (I) | 3.5 | length vs distance; intuitive comparison; direct comparison with endpoints aligned; comparison in improvised units | don't arrange many objects by length at once |
| **1M2** Money (I) | 6 | HK coins in circulation; read price tags **≤ $10**; count a group of coins ≤ $10; exchange within the 10/20/50-cent group and within the $1/2/5/10 group | no writing prices; no decimal place value; no exchange between the two groups; change problems are 2N5 |
| **1M3** Length and distance (II) | 4 | centimetre; measure and compare in cm; ruler use from the 0 mark; estimate with an "ever-ready ruler" | — |
| **1M4** Time (I) | 6 | o'clock and half past on an analogue clock; hour as a unit; intervals in whole hours ≤ 12; two problem types (finish time, interval); 7 days; 12 months; calendars | no drawing hands; no "a little after half past" |
| **1S1** 3-D shapes (I) | 6 | intuitive concepts of prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, spheres by touching, stacking, rolling; connect a 3-D object to its 2-D outline | no oblique shapes; no naming different prisms/pyramids |
| **1S2** 2-D shapes | 10 | points, straight lines, curves; draw and make them; triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, circles; **"form 2-D shapes by triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons and circles"** | properties of squares/rectangles are 2S4 |
| **1S3** Directions and positions (I) | 3.5 | "over, under, left, right, in front of, behind, between" relative to the observer; same words used to describe a picture | no complicated relative positions |
| **1F1** Inquiry and investigation | 10 | not a unit — a mode of working across the others | — |

Coming next (so nothing is built twice): P2 brings 3-digit numbers and `>`/`<`, **basic
multiplication (11.5 h)** and basic division, angles, quadrilaterals, pictograms; P3 brings
**fractions (I), 9.5 h**, capacity and weight, bar charts.

### 1.3 The three facts that set the whole scope

1. **Halves are a K3 milestone and a shape idea, not a fraction.** `[KG]` Appendix 2 puts "half of a
   piece" against "one full piece" at 5–6, and formal fractions wait for P3 (`3N5`, 9.5 h). The
   English and US systems say the same in different units. England's Year 1 statutory requirement is
   "recognise, find and name a half as one of two equal parts of an object, shape or quantity" and,
   in the same year, "recognise, find and name a quarter as one of four equal parts" — halves and
   quarters as *fractions of* discrete and continuous quantities, "by solving problems using shapes,
   objects and quantities"; Year 2 adds thirds, equivalence and unit fractions. CCSS `1.G.A.3`
   partitions circles and rectangles into two and four equal shares, noting that decomposing into
   more equal shares creates smaller shares. **The assessment is pointing at the shape strand, not
   the number strand.**
2. **The current app covers part of 1N2 and nothing else.** It has no counting, no numerals, no
   composition (1N1 — the unit P1 spends 13.5 hours on and calls "the foundation knowledge for
   performing addition with carrying and subtraction with borrowing"), no tens/units (1N3), no
   shape, no measure, no money, no position.
3. **Two of the twelve units are already served elsewhere in the estate.** 1M4 (o'clock, half past,
   days, months, calendar) is `clock.zt489.com`, whose tiers already cover o'clock, half past,
   quarters and the five-minute ring, and which has the day/week vocabulary as tier 0. Nothing in
   1M4 needs building again — only the calendar half (months, dates) is thin.

### 1.4 A commercial "K1–K2" assessment paper, compared with the above

Worth recording because it circulates as a benchmark. A MathConcept Education "First Steps" K1-2
booklet examined in October 2026 (50 items) contains: written addition to 58+5 and written subtraction
such as 21−3, 30−3, 13−5 (all needing carrying or borrowing); counting backward from 31 to 0; counting
in 5s and 10s from 0; writing the numerals 0–20 in a grid; mental dissection ("circle the shapes made
by these cut lines"); and "draw a line to cut the shape into half" on a cartoon animal head and on a
rotated kite.

Against the anchors: 1N2 caps addition at "within 18 verbally" and says "operations such as 12+5 and
17–5 are not required"; 1N4 adds two-digit carrying but states "subtraction doesn't include borrowing",
so 21−3 and 30−3 sit in **P2**; counting in 5s and 10s is **1N3 (P1)**; writing numerals to 20 is a
**K3 milestone** and the P1 unit 1N1; and "cut the shape in half" is **Year 1 / Grade 1** material —
HK does not introduce fractions until P3 (3N5, 9.5 h). A paper carrying a K1–K2 cover therefore spans
P1 to P2, and one of its "fractions" items is really a symmetry task.

Consequence for us: the paper is a tutoring-centre instrument, not an expectation of K2, and building
to it would be the pre-teaching the EDB prohibits. Its value is diagnostic only — what a child cannot
do on it is a signal about *that* child, not about the level to build at.

## 2. Teachable surface: what a supervised web app can and cannot carry

"App-markable" means the object on screen carries the answer, so the app can judge it. "Adult-marked"
means the child says or does something off-screen and the adult presses ✓/✗. "Paper/physical" means
the value is in the hand, not the screen.

| Content | Source unit | On-screen form | Marking | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Count a set and tap the numeral | KG 4–5, 1N1 | dots/objects → numeral strip | app | **build** — the biggest K gap |
| Match quantity ↔ numeral, both directions | KG 5–6, 1N1 | two-way matching | app | **build** |
| Compare quantities (more / fewer / same) | KG, 1N1 | two groups, choose | app | **build** |
| Count on / count back from a number | 1N1 | number line, tap the next | app | build |
| Ordinal words (first … tenth) | 1N1, 1S3 | a queue of objects | app | build |
| Odd / even to 20 | 1N1 | pair-up dots, spot the leftover | app | build |
| **Compose / decompose 2–18** ("5 is 3 and 2", "make 10") | 1N1 (core), EYFS bonds | part-whole bar, tap the parts | app | **build first in the number app** |
| Tens / units, count in 2s, 5s, 10s | 1N3 | base-ten blocks, grouped rows | app | build |
| Add/subtract within 18 | 1N2 | the current app | app | exists (plan I1–I5 fixes it) |
| Column form, carrying, borrowing | 1N4 | — | paper | **no** — paper skill, results < 100 |
| Length: longer/shorter, order by length | 1M1, KG 5–6 | pictures, choose/order | app | build (cheap tier) |
| Length in cm, ruler reading | 1M3 | on-screen ruler | app | marginal — one tier, honest about the motor gap |
| Improvised-unit measuring | 1M1 | objects as units | app | skip — the point is the hand |
| HK coins, price tags ≤ $10, coin exchange | 1M2 | coin images, tap to pay | app | **build** — high real-life value, HK-specific |
| Giving change | 2N5 (P2) | — | app | later |
| O'clock, half past, intervals, days/months | 1M4 | `clock.zt489.com` | app | exists; add calendar tier |
| 3-D shape recognition (prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone, sphere) | 1S1 | photos/renders, choose | app | build |
| 2-D shapes, lines, curves; **compose shapes from shapes** | 1S2 | tangram-style tap-to-place | app | build |

| Pictograms | 2D1 (P2) | data of shapes, count | app | later, cheap |

## 3. How the estate should expand

Four units of organisation, in the order they earn their place:

| # | App | Strand | Covers | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Maths drill** (`maths.zt489.com`) | equations | 1N2, then 1N1 composition as a mode | exists; 8-increment plan in flight (`specs/improvement-plan.md`) |
| 2 | **Shape & halving** (new) | shape & space | 1S1, 1S2, halves → quarters, 1S3 position, KG shape milestones | **next build** |
| 3 | **Clock** (`clock.zt489.com`) | time | 1M4 time-telling + calendar tier | exists |
| 4 | **Money** (new, later) | measure | 1M2 coins and price tags ≤ $10, later 2N5 change | deferred |

Deliberately not a fifth app: counting, comparing, odd/even and tens/units all belong to the number
strand and share one interaction grammar (show objects, tap a numeral or a choice). They are the next
tier of app 1 if it stretches to a topic menu, or app 5 if it does not — decided after the halving POC
is in a child's hands, not before.

**Why shape and halving is a separate app rather than a mode of the drill:**

- Different interaction: the answer is a *picture choice or a placed line*, not a keypad digit. The
  drill's entire state machine (one tap, auto-submit, 2 s advance) is built for numerals.
- Different judgement: a half is a property of a region; correctness needs geometry (equal area), which
  is a new pure module — and the one worth proving first, because a wrong area function is invisible.
- Different test strategy: hit-testing and SVG geometry need the browser layer. The clock app learned
  this the hard way — a 476-test green suite once certified a drag whose grab strokes lived outside the
  `<svg>`; one Playwright spec with `elementFromPoint` found it. Numbers do not need that; shapes do.
- Different timing: the drill has eight increments queued. Mixed into it, the halving work would either
  overtake them or sit at the back of the queue behind unrelated defect fixes.

Same shell, copied not shared: the design tokens (cream `#fff9c4`, teal `#4ecdc4`, 10 px radius,
emoji mascots), the preferences pattern, the celebration/goal contract, and the audio rule
(pre-recorded, English default plus Cantonese, because the kindergarten is Cantonese-medium).

## 4. The halves ladder, concretely

Target rung for a four-and-a-half-year-old, from the anchors: *see a shape and tell whether it is cut
in half* (KG 5–6 "half of a piece"; Y1/G1 as the school version). Not fractions, no symbols, no
numerals like 1/2.

| Rung | Question on screen | Marking | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| H0 Whole and parts | Tap the whole birthday cake, not the slice | app | the "one full piece" half of the KG milestone |
| H1 Same shape, different size | Which two pieces are the same shape? | app | the vocabulary halves need |
| H2 Equal vs unequal | Two pieces of the *same* shape — are the two parts the same size? | app | **this is the rung that teaches.** The non-example is the lesson |
| H3 Is this half? | One shape, one cut: does this show half? (vertical, horizontal, diagonal, and equal parts of a different shape) | app | the user's ask, as a discrimination |
| H4 Find the half | Four pictures of the same shape; tap the one showing half | app | production under choice; position must be shuffled |
| H5 Draw the cut | Tap the two ends of the line that cuts the shape into two equal pieces | app | **the school task** (Year 1 / Grade 1 wording: "draw a line to cut the shape into half"); judged on equal *area*, not symmetry — see the note below |
| H6 Half of a quantity | Share 6 sweets between 2 plates, both the same | app | discrete halves; the bridge to 1N1 composition |
| H7 Quarters | Cut in half twice; four equal parts | app | later, but closer than it looks: Year 1 and CCSS Grade 1 both include quarters — only thirds and equivalence wait for Year 2 / Grade 2 |

Traps this ladder exists to avoid, each of which a naive "half" page gets wrong:

- **The non-example must be the same shape cut unequally**, not a different shape, or the child learns
  "two pieces = half".
- **Diagonal halves of a square look nothing like vertical halves** and are still halves; a page that
  only ever shows vertical cuts teaches "half means a line down the middle".
- **More pieces ≠ more shape** — the CCSS progression names this explicitly ("decomposing into more
  equal shares creates smaller shares"); it needs its own discrimination before quarters.
- **Continuous and discrete halves are different ideas** (a piece of cake vs six sweets) — the
   distinction England's own guidance draws when it says pupils meet half and quarter as "fractions
   of" discrete and continuous quantities, connecting them to the equal sharing of sets. HK's K3
   milestone is the piece; keep sweets for H6.
- **A cut is not a drag.** Tap the region or tap the line; dragging a knife measures the wrist, not the
  concept.
- **"Half" is equal area, not a line of symmetry.** A rectangle has infinitely many halving lines and a
  square includes the diagonals; an app that judges "the" symmetry line marks correct cuts wrong. The
  judge needs an area test (within tolerance) on the regions the cut creates. The cartoon-head item is
  the opposite case: a symmetry task wearing a halves label.

## 5. Recommended sequence

1. **Finish the drill plan** as written (`docs/specs/improvement-plan.md`) — nothing here changes it;
   I1 is independent of the shape work. Its only scope addition, later, is a composition/bonds mode
   (1N1) because that is the documented prerequisite of the arithmetic it already teaches.
2. **Build the shape & halving app as a POC with H2 + H3 only** — two screens, one shape family
   (circle, square, rectangle), one playable level with audio, on a real URL, in a child's hands. Not a
   ladder, not a gate. Watch him: whether "same size" is a phrase he can act on, whether the unequal
   non-example teaches or confuses, and whether he wants to cut or to choose.
3. **Then** the rest of the shape strand (H4–H7, 1S1 3-D, 1S2 composition, 1S3 position), decided from
   what he did.
4. **Money last** — it is the most fun and the most asset-heavy (coin images at true relative size), and
   nothing else depends on it.

## 6. Open question

The concept assessment has not been seen here. It decides two things this map cannot: whether the
halves rung is H2 (equal/unequal) or H3 (is this half), and whether anything else was flagged beside
halves — number bonds and counting are the likely candidates given the K2/K3 boundary. Paste it, or
point at where it lives, and §5.2 gets sharper.
