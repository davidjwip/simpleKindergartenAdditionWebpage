# Hong Kong curriculum anchor: K1–K3 and Primary 1 mathematics

*Compiled by deepseek-v4-flash from EDB primary sources. Scope: one child, age 4.5, K2, Cantonese-medium kindergarten; horizon K1→P1. EDB sources only; market practice is marked as such.*

Keys: **[KG]** Kindergarten Education Curriculum Guide (2017); **[SUP]** Supplement… Learning Content of Primary Mathematics (2017); **[EN]** Explanatory Notes to Primary Mathematics Curriculum, Key Stage 1 (2018); **[KLA]** Mathematics Education KLA Curriculum Guide (2017); **[POA]** EDB "Primary One Admission for September 2027" leaflet. The maths KLA guide's actual title is *Primary 1 – Secondary 6*; P1 content sits in [SUP] and [EN].

## 1. What HK kindergartens are officially expected to do with number

The learning area is framed by one sentence: "To develop children's interest and motivation in learning mathematics through solving practical problems in real-life situations" ([KG], 2.6.3). Under it sit three learning objectives with "Examples of Learning Expectations" — examples, not graded targets. The number-relevant ones: "Display an interest in numbers and quantities in their environment"; "Understand the relationship between numbers and quantities, and be able to compare and tell the differences in quantity by using mathematical language"; "Express thoughts by using mathematical expressions such as more/less, long/short, addition/subtraction, front/back and left/right" ([KG], 2.6.3).

Pedagogy: "Teachers should make use of daily activities and play to introduce and consolidate mathematical concepts as appropriate", one attribute at a time, "progressing gradually"; and "One-way teaching and repetitive drilling on operations of mathematics can cause resentment. The activities should match children's interests" ([KG], 2.6.3).

The homework rules: "Do not require children in nursery class (K1) to hold a pencil and write"; "Do not require children in lower and upper kindergarten classes (K2 and K3) to do mechanical copying and calculation, and do not arrange homework that is excessive, frequent and too difficult, so as not to cause unnecessary pressure and drilling" ([KG], Chapter 4).

The only age-graded number expectations are developmental milestones in Appendix 2, not a syllabus:

- **4–5 years** (the 4.5 band): "Able to recite from one to twenty"; "Able to count up to ten objects".
- **5–6 years:** "Able to count and write from one to twenty"; "Able to do simple mathematical addition and subtraction" ([KG], Appendix 2).

So the official expectation at 4.5 is roughly *recite to 20, count ten objects*; at K3, *write* numerals to 20 and "simple" addition and subtraction, with no number range, no written algorithm and no fluency target. Any K1–K3 number scope-and-sequence is a school's or publisher's scheme, not EDB's.

## 2. Primary 1 mathematics: objectives and first-term emphasis

[SUP] §1 sets the P1–P3 Number Strand targets: "recognise the concepts of whole numbers* and simple fractions"; "perform four arithmetic operations of whole numbers and addition and subtraction of simple fractions"; "use numbers to formulate and solve simple problems" (whole numbers here mean non-negative integers).

**1N1 Numbers to 20 (13.5 hours)** — "recognise numbers 1-20"; "perform counting onwards and counting backwards"; "recognise the concepts of ordinal numbers and cardinal numbers"; "recognise the odd and even numbers up to 20"; "recognise the decomposition and composition of numbers 2-18". Bound tightly: students count "a group of not more than 20 objects"; "students are only required to perform counting by 1"; comparison is by one-to-one correspondence, and "In Primary One, students are not required to use the symbols '>' and '<' to record the comparison result" ([EN], 1N1). Objective 5 "only requires students to compose a number from two 1-digit numbers and decompose a number into two 1-digit numbers" ([EN], 1N1).

**1N2 Basic addition and subtraction (13.5 hours)** — "understand the basic concepts of addition and subtraction"; "perform addition and subtraction of numbers within 18 verbally"; "recognise the concept of 0"; "understand the relation between addition and subtraction"; "recognise the commutative property of addition". Three limits decide the level: students "verbally solve the problems presented mainly by diagrams and record their workings in horizontal form" ([SUP], 1N2); the problems "only involved addition and subtraction of those decomposition and composition number pairs within 18… That means operations such as 12+5 and 17–5 are not required"; and "students are not required to learn the column form" ([EN], 1N2). Even exploration is capped: "As the objective is not for drilling of calculation, the numbers involved in the related exploratory activities should be within 10" ([EN], 1N2).

**1N3 Numbers to 100 (6 hours)** — 21–100, units and tens place ("Teachers do not need to introduce the term 'place value'"), magnitude comparison, "counting in groups of 2, 5 and 10" with "Calculation… not required", estimation under 100 ([EN], 1N3; [SUP], 1N3). **1N4 Addition and subtraction (I) (13 hours)** — two- and three-number addition, associative property, subtraction, column form, word problems: "Addition includes carrying, but subtraction doesn't include borrowing"; results stay under 100; students "are not required to explain their calculation with statements" ([EN], 1N4).

**First term.** EDB publishes no term-by-term P1 scheme of work and refuses one: "the ordering of the Learning Units and Learning Objectives in the Supplement does not represent a prescribed sequence of learning and teaching" ([EN], Foreword). What the documents support: 1N1 and 1N2 lead the Primary 1 block of the contents list and of the "Flow chart showing the progression pathways for the learning units" ([SUP], Contents; Chapter 3), and carry 27 of the 285–356 hours for all of Key Stage 1 ([SUP], Chapter 2). A week-by-week "P1 term 1" is publisher or school pacing, not EDB's.

So P1 does not require > and <, column form in 1N2, borrowing, timed fact recall, times tables (2N3 is P2) or written explanations.

## 3. K3 → P1 transition, and what "interview practice" is worth

**Official: do not pre-teach.** "Kindergartens should not advance the primary school curriculum to kindergarten education and pre-teach the primary curriculum content in kindergartens" ([KG], 6.1), because "Requiring children to learn the primary curriculum content will undermine their learning interest and motivation, damage their confidence" ([KG], 6.3). Concretely, teachers "should not teach the primary school curriculum content in advance, for example, asking children to write difficult words with complicated strokes or do complex calculation exercises" ([KG], 6.3).

**Official: what P1 teachers expect.** "Children at the pre-primary level are expected to acquire preliminary mathematics concepts (e.g. counting and comparing) through hands-on activities, games, storytelling and daily life experiences…"; and "Teachers teaching Primary 1 should consolidate students' mathematical concepts in the 'Number' and 'Shape and Space' strands, which are the foundation knowledge for further studies" ([KLA], 3.6.1).

**Official: admissions.** For aided and government primaries the route is the POA System, whose stated purpose is "to eliminate the pressure imposed on young children by the intense competition to enter popular primary schools and the adverse effect this has on kindergarten education". At the discretionary-places stage: "Schools must not conduct tests or interviews on the applicant children" ([POA]). Selection uses a published points table — "First-born child… (5 points)", "Parent(s) or sibling(s) being a graduate of the primary school (10 points)" — then computerised Central Allocation. Mathematics performance enters nothing.

**Market / anecdotal, not official.** DSS, private and ESF primaries "are excluded from the POA System… Parents who wish their children to enroll in these schools may apply direct to the schools" ([POA]); no EDB document reviewed here describes their admission content. Claims about "P1 interview maths" therefore come from individual school practice, tutoring marketing or parent folklore. Interview drilling has no curricular warrant for the majority of P1 places.

## 4. Implications for a home drill app for a four-year-old

1. **The official ceiling at 4.5 is small.** Recite to 20, count ten objects ([KG], Appendix 2); compare groups in words, no > or < ([EN], 1N1). Ranging wider exceeds the documented band.
2. **Verbal and concrete, not written.** P1 addition is done "verbally" with workings "in horizontal form" ([SUP], 1N2). At 4.5 the app should prompt adult–child number talk over real objects, not grade the child.
3. **"Drill" is the framing the documents reject.** "Repetitive drilling… can cause resentment" ([KG], 2.6.3); K2/K3 must not get "mechanical copying and calculation" ([KG], Ch. 4); even P1 exploration is capped because the aim "is not for drilling of calculation" ([EN], 1N2). Design: short play rounds, one attribute, real-life contexts (snacks, stairs), no streaks, timers or scores.
4. **Build composition, not sums.** The key P1 prerequisite is 1N1 objective 5 — composing from two 1-digit numbers — "the foundation knowledge for performing addition with carrying and subtraction with borrowing in the future" ([EN], 1N1). For a four-year-old: "five is 3 and 2", "how many more to make 10".
5. **Do not build:** column form, carrying/borrowing, > and <, timed fact recall, two-digit written arithmetic, interview question banks — none is early-P1 content, and the last has no official basis for aided schools. **Safe to extend:** counting to 20 both ways, one-to-one correspondence, cardinality to 10, more/less/greater/smaller, ordinal words to tenth, odd/even to 20 as pattern, 2s/5s/10s grouping with objects (a 1N3 skill, playful at K3).
